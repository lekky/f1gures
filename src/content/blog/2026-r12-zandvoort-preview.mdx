A month off, and the season resumes with a goodbye.
[Zandvoort](/circuits/zandvoort/) leaves the calendar after 2026, so this
weekend is the last Dutch Grand Prix - the final orange invasion of the
dunes, the final laps around the banking, and, in a piece of scheduling
nobody planned, the first and only Zandvoort sprint. The circuit that
returned in 2021 as the home of a champion checks out with its hero seventh
in the standings, driving a car he described in Hungary as completely
broken.

The break froze the table at an awkward moment for everyone chasing.
<DriverChip ref="antonelli" /> sits on 219 with a 50-point lead;
[Hungary](/races/2026/11/) proved he can collect podiums even on his messy
weekends. The man arriving with momentum is <DriverChip ref="norris" /> -
pole and a win at the Hungaroring, his first Grand Prix victory since last
November, and a car that has been the field's fastest on Sundays for a
month. Thirty-three points a weekend are on the table here. Ninety-one
points is a lot of ground. It is not unreachable ground.

## One seat changes hands

The break's biggest news came from Red Bull, twice. First:
<DriverChip ref="hadjar" /> cracked a bone in his wrist training during the
shutdown and misses this weekend. <DriverChip ref="lawson" /> steps up from
RB to partner Verstappen - his first Red Bull start - while
<DriverChip ref="tsunoda" /> comes off the bench to take the RB seat
alongside <DriverChip ref="arvid_lindblad" />. Hadjar, sixth in Hungary and
the season's quiet overachiever, hopes to be back for Monza. Second:
<DriverChip ref="max_verstappen" /> signed a new contract through 2030,
ending a summer of exit-clause speculation in the least 2026 way possible -
committing his prime to a team currently fourth in the constructors'
standings.

<PullQuote>“I think I was closer to retiring than changing teams!”</PullQuote>

## What Zandvoort asks of the cars

Short lap, narrow ribbon, two banked corners - Hugenholtz at Turn 3 and the
18-degree final banking that slings cars onto the pit straight. Downforce
and commitment are everything; overtaking, as ever here, is mostly a
qualifying problem. The sprint format compresses all of it: one practice
session on Friday morning, then straight into sprint qualifying with dune
wind, sea air and a track that evolves lap by lap. The North Sea weather is
the wildcard - it has rained on this race weekend more often than not.
