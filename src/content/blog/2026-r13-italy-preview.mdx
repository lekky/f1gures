and Monza is about to stress-test both halves of it at once.
<DriverChip ref="norris" /> has won the last two Grands Prix from the last
two poles. <DriverChip ref="antonelli" /> - home race, freshly twenty, 59
points clear - is set to start from the back: Toto Wolff has confirmed
Mercedes will fit a fifth power unit of the season, one over the
allocation, at the circuit where a fresh engine and 200 mph slipstreams
make a recovery drive most plausible. The unit has already cost him a
retirement at [Barcelona](/races/2026/7/) and a wounded fifteenth at
[Silverstone](/races/2026/9/); Mercedes are trading Saturday for the
final ten rounds.

## What Monza asks of the 2026 cars

More than any circuit yet. The new power units split their output roughly
50/50 between combustion and electric, and Monza's long flat-out stretches
are exactly where the battery runs dry - "clipping", the deployment fading
before the braking zone, is expected to be the technical story of the
weekend. Teams that harvest efficiently through Parabolica and manage the
energy map down the straights will find lap time nowhere else offers; the
FIA can trim the race energy allowance at venues like this, and every team
has been simulating it for months. Verstappen's pre-season complaint about
the concept was aimed at precisely this weekend.

<PullQuote>“Being flat-out on the Monza straight and having to lift off the throttle 400 or 500 metres before braking is not the right direction at all.”</PullQuote>

Skinny wings, active aero at full stretch, and the year's biggest test of
straight-line efficiency: if the Red Bull-Ford unit really is the class of
the field, Monza is where it should finally show.
