The preview said Monaco was the best chance anyone had to stop
<DriverChip ref="antonelli" />. Instead he took [pole](/races/2026/6/) with a
1:12.051, led every lap, set the fastest lap of the race on lap 76, and won the
Monaco Grand Prix at nineteen. That is a grand slam, and it makes him the
youngest winner in the history of this race and the youngest driver ever to
complete one. It also made him the first Italian to take pole at Monaco since
Jarno Trulli in 2004.

The rest of the afternoon was considerably less tidy. Six cars retired, a red
flag stopped the race with fourteen laps to go, and the final podium position
was not settled until the following Friday.

## The race

<DriverChip ref="max_verstappen" /> had the best seat in the house and lost it
before the first lap was out. A power unit problem dropped the Red Bull through
the field from second on the grid, and he retired at the end of the opening
lap without completing a racing lap. Monaco's most valuable commodity is the
grid slot, and Red Bull's evaporated in about ninety seconds.

<DriverChip ref="antonelli" /> was never troubled. Out front from lights to
flag, he crossed the line 6.271s clear of <DriverChip ref="hamilton" />, who
had run a quiet, disciplined race from third and turned it into Ferrari's
second podium of the year.

The chaos arrived late. On lap 56 <DriverChip ref="stroll" /> crashed at the
final corner and brought out the safety car. When the race resumed,
<DriverChip ref="leclerc" /> crashed at the same corner on lap 64, ending his
home Grand Prix from fourth on the grid and prompting a red flag while the
marshals inspected the track surface. The race restarted from a standing start
with the order shuffled and the tyres cold.

<DriverChip ref="sainz" /> had two collisions in the minutes after that
restart, first with <DriverChip ref="hulkenberg" /> and then with
<DriverChip ref="colapinto" />, and parked the Williams with damage on lap 70
after being classified sixteenth. Hulkenberg took a ten-second penalty for the
first of them. <DriverChip ref="norris" /> had already gone, stopping on lap 43
reporting a loss of battery power, which is a modern way to lose a race at a
circuit that used to take your gearbox instead.

<DriverChip ref="russell" /> had the worst afternoon of anyone still running.
He reported engine braking and gear-selection problems, could not find a way
past <DriverChip ref="hadjar" />, picked up a five-second penalty for speeding
in the pit lane, and then collected a drive-through for failing to serve it
correctly. Sixth on the grid, twelfth at the flag, nothing scored, and a
championship lead handed further away.

## The podium that took five days

<DriverChip ref="gasly" /> crossed the line third, drove an excellent race from
ninth, and was then dropped to seventh by two separate five-second pit-lane
speeding penalties. Alpine petitioned for a Right of Review. On 12 June, five
days after the race, the stewards found the pit-lane speed calculation had used
an incorrect distance measurement, rescinded both penalties and reinstated him
to third. McLaren and Red Bull appealed; Mercedes lodged its own petition and
withdrew it on 18 June.

It stands as Gasly's sixth career podium and his first since São Paulo in 2024,
and it is Alpine's only visit to the rostrum since that afternoon.

Further back the penalties cascaded. <DriverChip ref="perez" /> took a
ten-second post-race penalty for being out of position at the standing restart,
which cost Cadillac what would have been their first Formula 1 point and
promoted <DriverChip ref="alonso" /> to tenth. From twenty-first on the grid,
that is Aston Martin's first point of 2026. <DriverChip ref="arvid_lindblad" />
came seventh from fifteenth in a strong afternoon for the rookie, with
<DriverChip ref="lawson" /> sixth ahead of him.

The weekend also marked McLaren's 1,000th Grand Prix entry, sixty years after
the team made its debut at this circuit in 1966.

Twenty-five more points for the leader and none for the man who was second in
the table pushes the gap to 66, the largest it has been all season.
<DriverChip ref="hamilton" /> inherits second on 90, one clear of
<DriverChip ref="russell" />, which is the first time in 2026 the number two
Mercedes has not been the closest challenger. <DriverChip ref="leclerc" /> stays
fourth on 75 despite scoring nothing, and <DriverChip ref="norris" /> and
<DriverChip ref="piastri" /> are locked together on 58.

Five wins from six, four poles, and a lead that has grown after every round
since March. [Barcelona](/races/2026/7/) is next, and unlike Monte Carlo it has
never been kind to a car that is merely well driven.
