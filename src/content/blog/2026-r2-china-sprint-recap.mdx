Nineteen laps into the sprint era of the new regulations, and the format has
already produced the best racing of 2026. The first sprint of the season went
to <DriverChip ref="russell" /> from sprint pole - but not before the lead
changed hands six times in five laps, a Ferrari nearly stole it at the flag,
and the championship picture picked up its first real controversy.

The fight at the front was not the one anyone predicted. It was
<DriverChip ref="hamilton" />, from fourth on the grid, who took the lead at
Turn 9 on the opening lap and traded it with <DriverChip ref="russell" /> six
times in five laps - including one pass around the outside of Turn 8 - at a
circuit he has won at six times. Russell settled it with a dive into the
Turn 14 hairpin on lap 5 and broke away; Hamilton had used his tyres up in
the fight and slipped back into the grasp of <DriverChip ref="leclerc" />,
who came past his new teammate on lap 8, wondering aloud on the radio whether
Hamilton knew how wide the 2026 cars are.

A safety car on lap 13 - <DriverChip ref="hulkenberg" />'s Audi stopped at
Turn 1 with an engine failure, moments after <DriverChip ref="bottas" />'s
Cadillac lost power - set up a three-lap dash to the flag. Ferrari
double-stacked for softs, Leclerc got wheelspin out of the hairpin at the
lap-16 restart, and that was the margin: 0.674s at the line, chasing hard,
never quite alongside. Russell admitted afterwards that Hamilton had caught
him off guard in those opening laps.

## Antonelli's messy Saturday

The polesitter's teammate had the sprint to forget.
<DriverChip ref="antonelli" /> bogged down from second on the grid, dropped
to the back half of the top ten, and barged into <DriverChip ref="hadjar" />'s
Red Bull at Turn 4 on the opening lap - a 10-second penalty that dropped him
out of the points after serving it in the safety-car pit sequence. He
recovered to fifth, 5.688s back. It could have been worse; it should have
been much better.

Behind, <DriverChip ref="lawson" /> and <DriverChip ref="bearman" /> rolled
the dice and stayed out under the safety car on their starting tyres, and the
gamble held: seventh and eighth, with a recovering
<DriverChip ref="max_verstappen" /> on fresh rubber finishing 0.3s short of
Bearman's point. Ninth, from eighth on the grid, after struggling for revs
at the launch - Verstappen called the Saturday a disaster, and the RB22's
weekend-long lack of grip is already the paddock's biggest story.
<DriverChip ref="albon" /> started from the pit lane - the first of 2026 -
after Williams broke parc fermé with a suspension change.
<DriverChip ref="arvid_lindblad" />'s afternoon was wrecked by an
opening-lap spin; the RB rookie was pulled in before the safety car.

Eight points stretch <DriverChip ref="russell" />'s perfect start - a win in
[Melbourne](/races/2026/1/), now a sprint win in Shanghai - to an 11-point
lead over <DriverChip ref="antonelli" />, with the Ferraris close enough to
matter. On this evidence the 2026 cars can genuinely race each other, and
windy-day tyre management is going to be a skill worth points all year.

The [Chinese Grand Prix](/races/2026/2/) itself runs on Sunday. Qualifying is
later today, and after that sprint, nobody at the front of this grid can
claim to know the order.
