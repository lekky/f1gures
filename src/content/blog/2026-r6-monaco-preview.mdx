Five rounds into the new regulations and the 2026 season has a shape: a
Mercedes at the front, a 19-year-old in it, and everyone else arguing over the
scraps. <DriverChip ref="antonelli" /> has won four of the first five races and
leads by 43 points. [Monaco](/circuits/monaco/) is where that kind of advantage
goes to be tested, because Monte Carlo is the one weekend a year when the
quickest car is only worth as much as the grid slot it converts.

Seventy-eight laps of a 3.337 km street circuit with barriers six inches off
the racing line. One DRS zone, nineteen corners, and an overtake that costs
more than it gains almost everywhere. The 2026 cars are narrower and lighter
than the machines that made this place a procession, which is the most
optimistic sentence anyone has written about racing at Monaco in a decade - but
Saturday will still decide most of Sunday.

Four wins and three poles from five for <DriverChip ref="antonelli" />, and a
Mercedes team 72 points clear of Ferrari. [Canada](/races/2026/5/) was the
messiest Sunday of the year - six retirements, <DriverChip ref="russell" /> and
<DriverChip ref="norris" /> both out - and the championship leader still walked
away with 25 more points. That is the pattern the chasers need broken, and
Monaco has broken better patterns than this one.

## What Monaco asks of the cars

Monaco inverts almost every priority of a normal weekend. **Mechanical grip
over aero:** the average speed is the lowest of the season, so what matters is
how the car rides kerbs and how much confidence it gives on turn-in at
30 mph, not how much load it makes at 180. **Front-end bite:** Loews is the
slowest corner in Formula 1 and the swimming-pool complex the most
commitment-dependent; a car that understeers is a car that finishes a second a
lap adrift. **Track position:** with one DRS zone and no run-off, the pit lane
decides more positions than the circuit does, and a safety car - Monaco
averages more than most - can hand the race to whoever happened to be in the
right window.

<PullQuote>“Everywhere else you drive the corner. Here you drive the barrier, and the corner happens somewhere in between.”</PullQuote>

The 2026 cars should help. Narrower bodywork and a shorter wheelbase mean more
room to place the car through Massenet and the Nouvelle Chicane, and active
aero has more effect at the low end of the speed range than DRS ever did. The
honest expectation is still that Saturday afternoon settles it.
